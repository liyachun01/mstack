# Personal infrastructure: value and potential service strategy

Date: 2026-10-01

Status: exploratory notes from a side conversation. These are hypotheses, not an approved business plan, validated pricing, or implementation instructions. This document does not change the main setup plan, authorize installations, or lift Basic Memory's deferral.

## Core conclusion

The infrastructure can help deliver a business; it is not necessarily the business.

Connecting MCP, memory, workflows, and a chat interface is not by itself a competitive advantage. A commercial offer needs a specific customer, an unresolved problem, and evidence that the result is worth paying for.

The decisive question is:

> After the customer already has WorkBuddy or another capable assistant, what painful problem still makes them call us?

If there is no convincing answer, this can remain a useful personal workshop without becoming a commercial product.

## Personal value, independent of commercialization

Potential benefits include less repeated explanation, easier project re-entry, reusable workflows, durable decisions, and more freedom to change harnesses without abandoning useful knowledge.

Those benefits need working habits and reliable integrations; installing components does not automatically create them. Local infrastructure also brings maintenance obligations, and local deployment does not automatically mean complete privacy: external models and connectors can still transmit data.

Prefer proving two or three useful workflows over accumulating more components. Judge success by time saved, fewer corrections, and ease of returning to work—not container count.

## Potential differentiation—not established advantages

### 1. A narrow workflow, understood deeply

An AI workspace for everyone is a difficult position. Helping a small iOS development team resume an old project, recover relevant decisions, and prepare a reviewed release checklist is a narrower hypothesis.

The value would come from understanding correctness, required approvals, exceptions, and costly manual work. Generic access to tools does not automatically solve these details.

### 2. Customer-controlled integration and reliable delivery

The user's preferences suggest an interest in project isolation, protecting existing services, reversible changes, portable configuration, and harness choice. This is a promising direction, not proof of superior engineering or a paying market.

A possible service would connect a customer's existing assistant, repositories, notes, and development environment, with documented ownership, tested recovery, and bounded support. Large vendors and partners can also provide integration; focus and responsiveness for a particular customer segment would need to earn the advantage.

### 3. Demonstrated portability

Harness-neutral context and workflows may help customers who change tools. Prove this through exports, recovery tests, and reconnection to another supported harness. Document what survives and what does not.

Open-source components alone do not establish portability, security, or privacy. The offering could complement WorkBuddy rather than replace it, subject to verified integration support.

## What is not a moat yet

More containers, multiple memory systems, embeddings, self-hosting, and a gateway are implementation choices. They may also increase support costs.

Potential longer-term defensibility would come from tested workflow knowledge, reusable delivery methods, an evaluation corpus, customer access, and trust. Customer-specific data must not become a reusable asset without appropriate permission.

## Service and pricing hypotheses

Start with a fixed-scope implementation on customer-owned infrastructure rather than immediately operating a multi-tenant SaaS.

Earlier discussion suggested the following exploratory prices, not researched market rates or recommended final quotes:

| Offer | Illustrative test range |
| --- | --- |
| DIY kit | RMB 199–699 |
| Personal setup | RMB 1,999–4,999 |
| Small-team implementation | RMB 8,000–30,000 |
| Bounded maintenance | RMB 499–1,999/month |

An illustrative RMB 3,999 pilot could cover one environment, one harness, one knowledge source, two agreed workflows, demonstrated backup/restore, onboarding, and bounded follow-up. Hardware, model usage, and vendor subscriptions would be separate.

These figures are not justified merely by installing tools. Validate an unresolved outcome first, then price against customer value, delivery effort, support burden, and third-party costs. Avoid unlimited support or vague promises of autonomous productivity.

## What a saleable offer would require

- Technical delivery: reproducible setup, pinned versions, upgrade/rollback procedures, tested backups, access boundaries, monitoring, and acceptance tests.
- Workflow materials / 场景包: reviewed prompts and skills, knowledge templates, routing rules, approval steps, and representative evaluation tasks.
- Runtime inputs / payloads: explicit project, task, sources, permissions, approval requirements, and retention expectations—not credentials embedded in prompts.
- Delivery materials / 交付物料: configuration repository, version inventory, data-flow explanation, ownership records, recovery/export handbook, acceptance report, and training.
- Sales materials / 销售物料: a narrow offer, a non-sensitive demo, credible before/after evidence, intake questionnaire, scope exclusions, and an honest privacy explanation.
- Commercial due diligence: component licenses, distribution and hosting rights, support terms, and data handling. Permission to self-host a component does not automatically settle every resale or managed-service arrangement.

A hosted SaaS would additionally require tenant isolation, billing, onboarding, account/data deletion, and ongoing operations. These are later considerations, not instructions to build now.

## Validation before more infrastructure

1. Choose a customer group that is reachable and whose work can be understood directly.
2. Interview three to five prospective customers; collect five representative real tasks and agree on success criteria.
3. Establish the simplest acceptable WorkBuddy-based baseline, including realistic setup effort.
4. Compare the proposed workflow on completion quality, human correction time, setup effort, recurring costs, and maintenance burden.
5. If a valuable gap remains, offer a bounded paid pilot. Track actual delivery time, support time, repeated usage, and willingness to continue paying.

Do not proceed on enthusiasm for the architecture alone. If the baseline is sufficient, consider helping customers adopt it instead. If the remaining gap is recurring and customers pay to close it, refine that narrow offer.

## Current recommendation

Explore specialist workflow implementation for small development teams, rather than a general AI assistant platform. This remains a hypothesis; there is no validated customer demand or proven moat yet.

The immediate business-learning step is finding a valuable residual problem—not adding another infrastructure component. Keep the personal project's benefits separate from pressure to monetize it.
